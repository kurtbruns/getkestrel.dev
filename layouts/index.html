How it works

Write, preview, schedule, send

Every post follows the same path, from a draft in Markdown to an email in your subscribers' inboxes and a permanent page on your own domain.

Write in Markdown

Draft a post in the web editor. If a draft changed under you, a stale save is rejected rather than clobbered.

Preview the exact email

See precisely what lands in the inbox: the rendered email HTML and plain-text, not an approximation. Send yourself a test first.

Schedule with a review window

Queue a send behind a cancelable countdown. Cancel it or move it any time before it fires; when the window closes, it sends on its own.

Send to a double opt-in list

Only confirmed subscribers receive it. The send loop is idempotent, so it never double-sends, and bounces and complaints suppress on their own.

Archived automatically

Each sent post becomes a permanent, indexable page on your domain. The same content stays readable long after the email is buried.

Working with robots

Ask Claude to do it

Kestrel is built on an API. Everything the app does, it does through that API. Connect Claude to your instance and it can do everything you can do in the editor. The same guardrails hold either way: a scheduled post waits out its review window, and you can still move it or cancel it.

Claude · Field Notes

Can you proofread my draft titled “First Frost” and schedule it to go out Friday at 7am?

  • Read draft · First Frost
  • Scheduled · Friday, 7:00 AM
Claude

One typo: “kestral” in the third paragraph, now “kestrel”. The rest reads clean.

Scheduled for Friday at 7:00 AM, cancelable until it sends.

Claude Code connects with a Cloudflare Access service token, and its edits are recorded as Claude, not as you. How to connect Claude →

Ownership

You keep what matters

When you have something to share, you should be able to reach the people who signed up to hear from you. With Kestrel you own that relationship: the list, the proof they opted in, the record of what you sent, and the archive it lives in.

The list

Your subscribers

They live in your own D1 database, not a vendor's. Query or export them any time. Nobody rents you access to your own audience.

The consent

Proof of permission

Every subscription is double opt-in, with the confirmation and the unsubscribe recorded. The evidence you had consent stays with you.

The delivery

An auditable record

Each send records who received which post, plus suppressions from hard bounces and spam complaints. You control the whole trail.

The archive

A permanent home

Every post gets a lasting public page on your own domain. The links you send never rot behind someone else's platform.

Under the hood

Built to be operated

Small, legible, and boring in the ways that matter. You run it on your own account and can read every line. It's open source under the MIT license.

TypeScript Cloudflare Workers D1 SES Resend
One API, two clients
A single HTTP API drives everything; the web editor and Claude are just clients of it. Nothing the editor can do is off-API.
Optimistic concurrency
Edits are revision-checked (If-Match / ETag), so a stale write is rejected with 409 instead of overwriting a newer draft.
Idempotent send loop
The scheduled sweep is safe to retry. It resumes a send without re-mailing anyone who already received the post.
Access-gated admin
Authoring sits behind Cloudflare Access. The reader pages are public, gated only by unguessable per-subscriber tokens.

    Step 2 · Run your own

    Deploy your own

    A step-by-step guide takes you to a live newsletter on your own domain. You'll need:

    • A Cloudflare account, with your domain's DNS on Cloudflare
    • An email provider: Resend or Amazon SES
    • Node.js 22 or later, and Git
    • A GitHub account, or another Git host, for your own copy of the repository

    No per-subscriber fees. A small list runs free; a large one costs your email provider's rates and Cloudflare's $5 Workers Paid plan.
