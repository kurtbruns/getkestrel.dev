How it works

Write, preview, schedule, send

Every post follows the same path, from a draft in Markdown to an email in your subscribers' inboxes and a permanent page on your own domain.

Write in Markdown

Draft a post in the web editor. If a draft changed under you, a stale save is rejected rather than clobbered.

Preview the exact email

See precisely what lands in the inbox: the rendered email HTML and plain-text, not an approximation. Send yourself a test first.

Schedule with a review window

Queue a send behind a cancelable countdown. Cancel it or move it any time before it fires; when the window closes, it sends on its own.

Send to confirmed subscribers

Only subscribers whose consent is on record receive it. Nobody is mailed twice, even if a send has to resume. A bounce stops mail to that address, and a spam complaint unsubscribes the reader, on their own.

Archived automatically

Each sent post becomes a permanent, indexable page on your domain, unless you send it as email only. Either way, the email's own link keeps working, so the post stays readable long after the email is buried.

Working with robots

Ask Claude to do it

Kestrel is built on an API. Everything the app does, it does through that API. Connect Claude to your instance and it works through that same API, as you, from proofreading a draft to scheduling its send. The same guardrails hold either way: a scheduled post waits out its review window, and you can still move it or cancel it.

Ownership

You keep what matters

When you have something to share, you should be able to reach the people who signed up to hear from you. With Kestrel you own that relationship: the list, the proof they opted in, the record of what you sent, and the archive it lives in.

The list

Your subscribers

They live in your own D1 database, not a vendor's. Query or export them any time. Nobody rents you access to your own audience.

The consent

Proof of permission

Signups are double opt-in, and every import that adds subscribers carries your word that they opted in. Confirmations and unsubscribes are recorded, so the evidence you had consent stays with you.

The delivery

An auditable record

Each send records who received which post, and every bounce and spam complaint it drew. You control the whole trail.

The archive

A permanent home

Every post gets a lasting page on your own domain: in your public archive, or at an unlisted link if you send it as email only. The links you send never rot behind someone else's platform.

Moving from another service? Bring your list with you: import it in the dashboard, or ask Claude to move it with its /migrate-list skill. Your readers arrive confirmed, on your word that they opted in, and the import emails no one. Bring your existing list →

Under the hood

Built to be operated

Small, legible, and boring in the ways that matter. You run it on your own account and can read every line. It's open source under the MIT license.

TypeScript Cloudflare Workers D1 Amazon SES
One API, two clients
A single HTTP API drives everything; the web editor and Claude are just clients of it. Nothing the editor can do is off-API.
Optimistic concurrency
Edits are revision-checked (If-Match / ETag), so a stale write is rejected with 409 instead of overwriting a newer draft.
Idempotent send loop
The scheduled sweep is safe to retry. It resumes a send without re-mailing anyone who already received the post.
Access-gated admin
Authoring sits behind Cloudflare Access. The reader pages are public, gated only by unguessable per-subscriber tokens.

  2. 2

    Step 2 · Run your own

    Deploy your own

    Kestrel installs from npm as @kurtbruns/kestrel. Your instance is a small repository of your own that names the release it runs, so upgrading is installing a newer one. A step-by-step guide takes you from an empty repository to a live newsletter on your own domain. You'll need:

    • A Cloudflare account, with your domain's DNS on Cloudflare
    • An AWS account, to send through Amazon SES
    • Node.js 22 or later, and Git
    • A GitHub account, or another Git host, for your instance's repository

    No per-subscriber fees. You pay Amazon SES's rates per email. Cloudflare's free Workers plan suits a list of up to about 1,000 subscribers, and sends to all of them in about 5 minutes once AWS raises your SES send rate to 50 a second, which costs nothing. A larger list belongs on Cloudflare's $5 Workers Paid plan, for the database's daily writes as well as faster sends.

    Open the setup guide
